## Why the failed economic test matters

A study can reduce calls to an expensive model while introducing additional specialized calls, orchestration and time. A favorable comparison on one dimension does not satisfy an overall preregistered economic threshold. ANANKE therefore explicitly distinguishes:
- Frontier-model call count from **all-provider model call count**.
- Provider-pricing estimates from **actual invoiced spend**.
- Token avoidance from **net compute or energy savings**.
- Offline/qualification behavior from **live customer integration**.
- Correctness on sampled tasks from **general safety claims**.

## Next decisive evidence

A qualified product result should show:
1. Authorized end-user/project identity and actual durable receipt storage.
2. An independently justified task needing real inference.
3. Actual provider call with normalized raw token/usage accounting.
4. A result that qualifies as reusable Capital, and a later separate request that validly reuses it.
5. An affected result invalidated after source change while unrelated work remains eligible.
6. Legitimate denials, scoped isolation, credential revocation and cleanup.
7. A matched customer workload baseline showing quality, latency, provider cost and governance overhead.

Until then, user-facing accounts, bills, dashboards, ROI, Capital effectiveness and beta readiness remain **unproven** in the current integrated candidate.
