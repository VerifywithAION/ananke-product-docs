# ANANKE | Investment thesis

**Category thesis:** Inference Necessity Infrastructure / Intelligence Expenditure Infrastructure.

**One-line pitch:** ANANKE seeks to turn unnecessary model recomputation into governed, evidence-backed reuse while reserving fresh inference for work that genuinely needs it.

## 1. The pain is structural

The dominant interface to AI compute remains a fresh request. Routing, caching, batching and prompt optimization can make that request cheaper; none of these alone guarantees the request was necessary. In an enterprise with multiple agents and repetitive procedures, execution volume may grow faster than meaningful novelty.

The problem is not simply tokens. It is **spending new intelligence on obligations already defensibly satisfied**, or conversely reusing an answer that has become wrong, stale or unauthorized.

## 2. The proposed product shift

ANANKE's governing question is **necessity before execution**. It aims to evaluate whether existing validated computation, ordinary deterministic work or authorized fresh models can satisfy each obligation under source, identity, freshness, safety and policy boundaries.

When reuse is independently justified, the prior result is treated as **Computational Capital**: an asset with eligibility limits, not an unrestricted memory. If conditions changed or evidence is insufficient, the safe option is fresh execution or governed refusal.

## 3. The initial commercial wedge

Prioritize workloads with **high recurrence, well-defined correctness and explicit change events**, not every conceivable model prompt. Strong early pilot candidates include policy-backed support classification, versioned document processing and recurring agent checks. Buyers would be AI-platform leaders, infrastructure/FinOps teams and enterprise process owners with measurable inference bills and quality controls.

The validated customer category and willingness to pay remain open questions; they must be proved in pilots, not inferred from laboratory reductions.

## 4. Evidence that creates the right to attempt a product

- **EXP005F:** local measured tokens 140,060 → 724 and calls 1,000 → 5 in a defined repeatable workload.
- **EXP025A_R5:** 70,650 → 2,836 measured tokens, calls 500 → 20, bounded utility and zero false reuse reported within that test.
- **ANANKE × JEV A3:** 600 of 1,000 defined obligations resolved before specialized-model dispatch in a real-provider comparison.
- **Four-Arm R1:** favorable frontier-call and task-correctness observations, **but overall preregistered economic acceptance FAIL**.
- **Engineering:** governed-runtime, validated-reuse, project/control-plane, website and minimum-intelligence candidates developed in stages.

All findings are **scoped**. None establishes integrated customer ROI, total energy reduction, revenue, or currently qualified production behavior.

## 5. Potential long-term product advantages—to be proved

1. **Economic switching costs:** if workflows accumulate valid reusable computation, value might grow with preserved applicability. This is a hypothesis, not measured network effects.
2. **Trust boundaries:** correct invalidation, authorization and receipts could matter as much as raw hit rate in regulated operations.
3. **Provider complementarity:** a necessity decision can sit logically before multiple vendors and complement existing routers/caches.
4. **Measurement:** a credible net-cost ledger could connect saved recomputation to customer economics.

These require technical, rights, safety and customer validation. They are not established competitive moats today.

## 6. Revenue and GTM hypotheses

Possible packaging options to test: project/workspace subscription, governed request orchestration, retained Capital and audit features, enterprise support and deployment controls. Pricing should reward **net customer value and governance**, not encourage higher model consumption. Provider charges and ANANKE charges must remain separable.

No public price, ARR, committed contract, paying customer or fundraising metric is claimed.

## 7. The make-or-break milestone

The next evidence milestone is not a bigger ratio. It is a **real, authorized, customer-scoped end-to-end flow** from the existing product interface through the accepted governance engine and persisted accounting, followed by a controlled model-backed necessity/Capital sequence on distinct requests and a matched baseline. It must show quality, false-reuse controls, real tokens, provider calls, latency, governance overhead and net cost.

As of 20 November 2026, that combined product milestone remains unaccepted and beta is **NO-GO**.

## 8. Investment risks

- Benefit may be limited to a narrow set of highly repeatable workloads.
- Governance overhead can erase provider savings.
- Source/identity correctness failures can be more expensive than needless inference.
- Fast-moving provider pricing, prompt caching and router products can compress differentiation.
- Intellectual property and freedom-to-operate require specialist review.
- Commercial adoption, distribution and monetization are unproven.
- Integrated product qualification and external security/operations review remain open.

## 9. What would justify a stronger claim?

Independent workload measurement, a safety and quality baseline, current E2E acceptance, reproducible net economic improvement, customer willingness to pay, repeat deployment and retention. Until then, ANANKE's strongest defensible description is **research-backed product engineering with a falsifiable infrastructure thesis**.
