## Slide 01 — ANANKE
**Headline:** Intelligence should be spent only when it is necessary.

Inference Necessity Infrastructure. A governed layer designed to decide when fresh model computation is required, when validated work can be reused, and how actual execution is accounted for.

**Speaker note:** ANANKE does not seek to be the next language model. It seeks to help systems decide whether they need another one.

## Slide 02 — The wasted-work problem
**Headline:** More AI requests do not always mean more new intelligence.

Agents and enterprise workflows revisit unchanged or previously completed obligations. Routing and prompt optimization lower the cost of execution but may not prove whether execution is needed. Blind reuse, however, can introduce stale or unauthorized answers.

**Visual suggestion:** request volume vs genuinely novel work; conceptual, not measured chart.

**Competition context (checked 24 November 2026):** existing gateways already offer semantic caches; task-aware caching and validation have published prior art. See [Competitive Proof Matrix](competitive-proof-matrix.md) and [public prior-art landscape](../research/prior-art-public-landscape.md). Do not convert a proposed distinction into a claim of exclusivity or matched commercial superiority.

## Slide 03 — The category opportunity
**Headline:** Necessity governance belongs before inference optimization.

Optimize **whether** a new model call is justified, then **which** executor is sufficient and authorized. Treat the result as governed work, not an unqualified cache entry.

## Slide 04 — The product
**Headline:** Preserve validated work. Recompute only when justified.

A requested workflow can be resolved by authorized deterministic work, validated existing computation, or appropriately admitted fresh intelligence. A receipt communicates the decision. **This depicts intended product behavior**, not currently certified end-to-end operation.

## Slide 05 — Computational Capital
**Headline:** Completed work can become a reusable asset.

A previous result earns limited reuse eligibility only while its evidence, source state, scope and applicable authority remain valid. When inputs change, affected reuse must stop. Computational Capital is a product concept, not an accounting asset valuation.

## Slide 06 — Who benefits
**Headline:** Start with repeatable, high-accountability workflows.

Three proposed pilots: governed support classification; versioned document checking; and repeated development-agent tasks. Expand only where baseline quality, scope, change events and net economics support reuse.

## Slide 07 — Scientific origins
**Headline:** Measured experiments motivate the product.

- EXP005F, local Ollama: **1,000 → 5 calls**, **140,060 → 724 tokens** in its fixed workload.
- EXP025A_R5: **500 → 20 calls**, **70,650 → 2,836 tokens**.
- ANANKE × JEV A3: **600/1,000** obligations resolved upstream before specialized-model execution.

**Footnote:** Separate experiments, noncomparable baselines; not live customer savings or universal performance claims.

## Slide 08 — We also publish the failures
**Headline:** Research discipline is part of the investment case.

A frozen four-arm test reduced frontier dispatches (101 vs 240 direct), but **failed its preregistered overall economic criteria**. This matters: reducing frontier calls alone is not proof of positive net economics.

## Slide 09 — Built vs not yet qualified
**Headline:** Research and component engineering exist. Integrated product acceptance is next.

Local model experiments, provider comparison studies, governed-component work, control-plane/product surfaces and focused M06 tests are documented. A complete current customer-scoped native M01–M08 plus real model-backed Capital and dashboard accounting is **not proven**. No public beta is live.

## Slide 10 — Commercial hypothesis
**Headline:** Sell verifiable reduction in unnecessary work—not more tokens.

Candidate offerings include governed request management, auditable Capital, organizational controls and enterprise support. Demonstrate cost per correct obligation, quality guardrails, latency and total governance overhead before finalizing pricing.

## Slide 11 — Execution roadmap
**Headline:** Prove product truth before scaling distribution.

P0: authorized current local customer acceptance. P1: real provider/Capital and net economics. P2: safe pilot and repeatable developer onboarding. P3: enterprise reliability and controlled rollout. P4: qualified expansion across executors and potential future research products. No unverified launch dates.

## Slide 12 — The ask
**Headline:** Evaluate the thesis on real, versioned, repetitive workloads.

The current next milestone is a narrowly scoped end-to-end acceptance and measured pilot design. Interested partners and investors can inspect the public research boundaries, propose a qualifying workload and discuss diligence with repository maintainers. **No fundraising round, financing target or commercial availability is announced here.**
