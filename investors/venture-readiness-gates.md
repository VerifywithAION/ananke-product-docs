# ANANKE | Venture diligence — proof gates and evidence gaps

**Company evidence hardening plan · 24 November 2026 · Public-safe version**

This is a **readiness test**, not a score of company value, valuation, investment advice, a commercial performance certification or a substitute for a private investor data room. No staffing names, private repository details, security internals, cap table, sales records or unverified investor metrics are represented.

## 1. First decide what is being invested in

**Current externally supportable description:** a research-backed ANANKE product effort focused on the necessity of AI inference, with real bounded studies and candidate governance/product engineering.

**Current investment-stage constraint:** no accepted full customer E2E and no measured commercial product economics as of [24 November 2026](../STATUS.md). No audited company/entity, revenue, team, paying customers or fundraise is disclosed in [company.md](../company.md).

A VC needs two different proofs: **the technology can do something useful** and **customers will pay enough for that usefulness to support a durable business**. Laboratory token ratios establish neither on their own.

## 3. Three gates that matter before a venture-scale story

### Gate A — Product truth

A real authorized customer workflow must make a necessity judgment, correctly admit genuine model work when required, preserve independently qualified work where appropriate, deny stale/incorrect reuse, store durable project-scoped receipts and report actual provider calls/tokens. Include failures, identity isolation and recovery.

**Today:** not fully accepted. This is the bottleneck, not a missing deck design element.

### Gate B — Commercial truth

Measure one actual customer's baseline cost for a **correct** resolved obligation against a governed workflow, including quality/false reuse, *all* model calls, validation/identity overhead, retries, latency and storage. Repeat across temporal source changes.

**Today:** absent for complete integrated product. Research ratios cannot fill the gap.

### Gate C — Market truth

Independent prospective buyers acknowledge the problem, try an integrated product, and show meaningful willingness to pay or recurring usage after a representative observation period. Negative feedback and lost prospects belong in the confidential data room.

**Today:** no verified public cohort or paying-customer record provided.

## 4. Why now — research questions, not slogans

A persuasive **Why Now** should test these competing explanations:

- Agentic applications and repeated enterprise workflows may increase duplicated obligations, creating demand for a **necessity** layer.
- Meanwhile, native provider caching, more efficient inference servers, cheaper specialized models and improved gateway-level caching might **shrink** the incremental economic opportunity.
- Governance pressure may increase willingness to pay for controlled, auditable reuse; alternatively, extra validation and procurement complexity might make the product harder to adopt.

**Needed data:** source-dated third-party workload adoption and provider-pricing evidence; actual buyer interviews; change/novelty composition; live competitor feature verification and a pilot demonstrating positive net economics.

**Do not** infer enterprise need or urgency solely from broad AI adoption headlines or general AI market spending.

## 5. A buyer-driven initial segmentation proposal

| Hypothesis | What to ask a real buyer | Why it is testable | Main risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Policy-backed support classification | What fraction of decisions genuinely repeat under unchanged policies? | Versioned policy decisions and correct classifications can be audited | High exception rate / stale authority |
| Recurring document review | What changes between document and policy revisions? | Explicit versioning and gold-standard review set | Too little eligible reuse after risk checks |
| Repeated developer-agent work | How often are files, dependencies and obligations unchanged? | Commits and source revisions can be controlled | Cheap local model or existing cache suffices |

Buyer interviews should be recorded as evidence, not presented as existing accounts until actually conducted.

## 6. Market sizing without invented TAM

Use a **bottom-up** rather than top-down model:

**Eligible annual governed obligations** = reachable organizations × relevant workflows per organization × annual obligations per workflow × verified eligibility fraction.

**Serviceable annual value pool** = eligible annual obligations × net avoidable spend per correct obligation × realistically capturable share, subject to quality, security and adoption.

**Potential ANANKE revenue** = number of *paying* organizations × evidenced annual contract value or separately justified usage pricing.

Every factor needs observed denominators, source date, confidence and sensitivity bands. A vast AI market does not imply ANANKE has a defensible multi-billion-dollar outcome.

## 7. Hiring and spend should follow evidence

**Now / product gate:** accountable full-stack/cloud integration owner, independent security review capability, and product acceptance/QA capacity—not a speculative global sales force.

**After accepted pilot:** developer integration/support owner and customer discovery/solutions capability.

**After demonstrated recurring demand:** enterprise sales capacity, support operations, procurement/compliance, reliability and financial controls.

These describe **needed functions**, not current employees or announced hires. Round budgets should be modeled against **actual salaries, jurisdiction, existing ownership and measured burn** in a confidential board/founder record—not published as a company-wide ask without authorization.

## 8. What an institutional investor should not be able to catch us inventing

- A claim that ANANKE has **no competing prior art** or that cached-response validation is novel by itself.
- A product-feature comparison based on rival roadmaps while treating ANANKE's future as live.
- A token-reduction figure presented as audited net customer savings.
- Ignoring the economic FAIL from the four-arm experiment.
- A claimed provider partnership from an external benchmark.
- A declared beta, signed contract, board, founder team or financing round without primary records.
- A “secret gist” used as if it were a confidential data room.

**Confidential research:** patent counsel claim charts, FTO and legal priority strategy; private security material; cap table, burn, customer data/consents, contractual diligence, employee identity and signed investor information.

## 9. Short milestone sequence, not a release-date promise

1. **Close P0 customer authority/receipt acceptance**, documenting exact negative cases and the same production-candidate route.
2. **Close P1 qualified model-backed Capital and net value**, ensuring that novelty and source changes are handled safely.
3. **Execute a bounded competitor trial** on a fair, preregistered customer problem with direct model, gateway cache and semantic-cache baselines.
4. **Validate the buyer and budget** with real interviews and paid or permissioned pilots, clearly reported as such.
5. **Build an investment data room** with validated legal/company ownership, team, technical evidence, credible bottom-up segment sizing, financing scenario and security review, all controlled for confidentiality.
6. **Choose the investor type and round only after the actual product and commercial evidence justify it**.
